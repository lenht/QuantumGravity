A note on what's below, and what isn't. The book's own reference chapter collects formulae for only three of its six theories: Space Particle Dualism, Entropic Expansion Cosmology, and Chromogravity & Electrogravity. Charge Mass Dualism, the Similar-Worlds Interpretation, and Conscious Set Theory have no equivalent table in that chapter — their numbers live in the prose of their own chapters instead, which is where this site's deep-dive pages for them already draw from. This page doesn't fill that gap; it transcribes what the reference chapter itself contains, and no more.

How to read these tables. The "Value" column is the book's own — usually more decimal places than the rounded figures used in the six theory pages' own prose, since this is a reference chapter rather than a narrative one. Where a value reads "Any," the book means the formula is a general law rather than one with a single fixed output (Newton's law of gravity is "any" in the same sense). The year in each entry's name is the book's own dating convention, carried over from every theory page's "Development notes" section; a second year (e.g. "2018 & 2020") means the formula was revised. Where a formula in this chapter differs from a figure already stated elsewhere on this site, that's noted under the relevant table rather than silently resolved one way or the other.

Space Particle Dualism

Thirteen entries, from the elementary-space radius formula through the lifetime of the shortest-lived virtual particle it allows. Most of the critical-frequency chain underlies the slit experiment SPD proposes; the granular-dimensionality row is the exact figure SPD's own hierarchy-problem section and Charge Mass Dualism's recount both already point to.

Theory 4 of 6 · the chain behind the 42-trillion-year figure

Entropic Expansion

Six entries. The middle three are the exact machinery behind EEC's own post-decoupling age derivation — the time-redshift constant here (39,625,004,647 lyr) is the same one the homepage's own formula card and Charge Mass Dualism's "16-trillion-year dark epoch" aside both already assume.

Entropic Expansion — every formula in the book's reference chapter for this theory
Name Description Formula Value
Initial mass of primordial exotic holes (2017 & 2018) Relevant for the precise version of the time-redshift equation. 1,125,587.541 kg
Impulse of exotic matter particles dependent on host black hole (2018) Only of theoretical value. Always practically zero.
Hubble parameter at different times (2019) According to SPD, the expansion of the universe is independent of gravity. Any.
Time-redshift equation (2019; 2021) Allows the light-travel distance to any object to be calculated. ≅ z × 39,625,004,647 yr
Initial Hubble parameter (2019) Impacts only the seventh digit in the age of the universe. 2.750307987×10-17 m/s per light-second
Initial temperature of the universe (2015) The initial temperature depends on the average entropy density. 5,963.0497688 K

Two things worth flagging rather than smoothing over. First, "initial mass of primordial exotic holes" (1,125,587.541 kg) is a different quantity from the minimum mass for a primordial black hole to close a horizon that EEC's own primordial-black-holes section derives (about 9.80×1030 kg, roughly 4.9 solar masses) — the similar name is easy to conflate with a very different number. Second, this table's "initial temperature" formula is dated 2015, predates the supermassive-black-hole survey EEC's own full-age section is built on, and its result (5,963 K) matches none of that section's own figures (the 3,418–11,712 K survey range, or the 4,021 K BAO-tuned pick) — this reference-chapter formula looks superseded by the later method, the same way EEC's page already flags an earlier, "now-superseded" age estimate elsewhere. The formula also prints T on both sides (Ti on the left, a plain T inside the cube root on the right); that's transcribed exactly as the book's own table shows it, not a typo introduced here. The initial-Hubble-parameter row's own MSBHμ is likewise printed exactly as given — the book's reference chapter doesn't define it within this table, and this guide couldn't trace where it's defined elsewhere.

Theory 6 of 6 · the book's own chapter groups this table under SPD, not on its own

SPD-Chromogravity and Electrogravity

Seven entries, building directly on Space Particle Dualism's own constants rather than starting fresh — the book's own table name, "SPD-chromogravity," makes that dependency explicit in a way the theory page's own prose only implies. The last row, the general formula for any element's own gravitational constant, is the one Chromogravity's Cavendish meta-analysis applies across the whole periodic table.

SPD-Chromogravity and Electrogravity — every formula in the book's reference chapter for this theory
Name Description Formula Value
Mass-gravity constant of the proton (2018; 2020) The basis of the chromogravity constant. Gp = 6.617310×10-11 m3 kg-1 s-2
Chromogravity constant (2018) Not used in combination with a mass, but with quark count. 2.05700×10-65 m3 kg s-2
Electrogravity constant (2018) Analogous to the above. 1.35096×10-66 m3 kg s-2
Law of chromogravity (2018) Replaces Newton's law of gravity. Any.
Law of electrogravity (2018) Analogous to the above. Any.
Mass-gravity constant of hydrogen (2018; 2020) The basis of all mass-gravity constants. GH = 6.613431×10-11 m3 kg-1 s-2
Mass-gravity constant of any element (2018; 2020) The gravity per kilogram depends on the chemical composition. Any.

Worth flagging: this table's GH (6.613431) and Gp (6.617310) are close to, but not identical with, the "refined" values Chromogravity's own page states in prose — GH = 6.613511 and Gp = 6.617210. That's the same "two bases" split the Cavendish section's own predicted-G table note already catches: a per-material table reproduces cleanly from the steel-anchored GH = 6.613430 (which this reference table's 6.613431 matches, to rounding), not from the refined 6.613511 quoted elsewhere in the same chapter. The chromogravity and electrogravity constants (2.05700×10-65 and 1.35096×10-66) do match Chromogravity's own prose values exactly.
